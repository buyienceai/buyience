Two things fall out of this table immediately.

The gap between tiers is enormous. Entry B2B tooling runs $39–£135/month. Mid-market runs $1,499–$2,500/month. Enterprise runs $40,000+/year before implementation. There is no gentle slope — there are three bands with large gaps between them.

"Custom pricing" usually means expensive. If a platform won't publish a number, it's rarely because the number is low.

For an SMB distributor, this means the practical question isn't "which platform is best" but "which band am I actually in, and what's genuinely available there."

## How to narrow the field fast

Four questions eliminate most of the market in about ten minutes.

1. **Does your revenue arrive through a catalogue or a quote?** If buyers log in and order known products at agreed prices, you want catalogue-led commerce. If customers describe a requirement and you send a quote that gets negotiated, you want quoting-led commerce. This single distinction matters more than any feature comparison, and most platforms are clearly one or the other.
2. **Does your ERP govern pricing and credit?** If your ERP is the source of truth and the storefront should reflect it rather than hold its own logic, that points toward ERP-led platforms such as Sana Commerce, and away from platforms expecting to own pricing themselves.
3. **Do you sell DTC as well as wholesale?** If yes, platforms that run both from one catalogue and inventory pool — Shopify and BigCommerce — have a structural advantage that B2B-only platforms can't match.
4. **What's your realistic monthly budget?** Honestly. If it's under $500/month, enterprise platforms are not a long shot, they're out, and demos with them waste weeks.

## The shortlist by fit

### Catalogue-led wholesale: BigCommerce B2B or Shopify

If your buyers order from a catalogue at their agreed prices, these two are the default and the competition between them is close.

BigCommerce has the distinction of publishing its pricing, and its B2B capabilities include price lists, quotes, approvals and ERP connectivity. The B2B-ready Performance tier starts at $1,499/month, with custom pricing above $1M in annual sales.

Shopify became substantially more credible for B2B in 2026, when company accounts, customer-specific pricing catalogues, net payment terms, PO numbers and quick order lists moved to every paid plan rather than remaining Plus-only. If you're already on Shopify for DTC, this is now a genuinely strong option without a Plus upgrade, depending on your requirements. For a deeper look at where Shopify fits versus quoting-led platforms, see our [Buyience vs Shopify Plus comparison](/blog/buyience-vs-shopify-plus-for-b2b-which-fits-wholesale-distributors).

Rule these in if wholesale means self-service ordering. Rule them out if your deals are negotiated line by line.

### ERP-governed distribution: Sana Commerce and similar

If your ERP already holds pricing, credit limits, and inventory as the single source of truth, platforms built to surface ERP data rather than duplicate it reduce integration pain considerably. This is a genuinely different architecture, and for distributors with a mature ERP it's often the right answer regardless of what the storefront looks like.

### Enterprise complexity with budget: OroCommerce, Adobe Commerce

OroCommerce is built around RFQ, account hierarchies and multi-org structures, and is widely recommended for distributors with complex account relationships. Adobe Commerce offers deep native workflows for quoting, approvals and requisition lists.

Both are credible for genuine enterprise complexity. Both are out of reach for most SMB distributors — Oro starts around $22,000/year on-premise and scales well past $200,000, and Adobe Commerce implementations are substantial projects, not configurations.

If you're an SMB distributor, the useful thing to know about these platforms is that they exist and you probably can't afford them yet. That's not a criticism of either product.

### Budget-constrained: WooCommerce plus extensions, B2B Wave

If budget is the binding constraint, WooCommerce with wholesale extensions is the low-cost route — free core plugin, with B2B behaviour added through separately licensed extensions. B2B Wave publishes £135/month for its Pro and Scale tiers.

The honest trade-off is that you're assembling capability from parts and accepting more maintenance work in exchange for lower cost. For a distributor with simple pricing and a small catalogue, that can be entirely reasonable.

This is where we sit, and the honest framing is narrow rather than broad.

If most of your revenue arrives through quotes that get negotiated — common in fastener and MRO distribution, industrial supply, and custom or configured products — then catalogue-led platforms leave your actual sales process outside the system. The negotiation happens in email, the margin decisions happen on instinct, and the platform records outcomes rather than supporting the work. That gap is the same one we cover in [why manual B2B quoting costs you deals](/blog/why-manual-b2b-quoting-is-costing-you-deals).

## What to ignore while evaluating

**G2 scores in isolation.** Most platforms cluster between 4.0 and 4.5. The spread is noise relative to fit.

**Feature-count comparisons.** Every platform lists company accounts, custom pricing and net terms. The question is whether the architecture matches your sales motion, which a feature table cannot show you.

**Any list — including this one — that concludes its own publisher is best overall.** Read for the facts, discount the verdict.

## The one question that matters most

Pull last quarter's orders. What share came from a buyer selecting known products at known prices, versus a buyer describing a requirement and receiving a quote that was then discussed?

That ratio points at your platform category more reliably than any comparison table, and it's the question most evaluation processes skip.

## FAQ: Choosing a B2B Platform as an SMB Distributor

### How much do B2B ecommerce platforms actually cost?

Published entry points as of late 2026 include BigCommerce Core at $39/month and its B2B-ready Performance tier from $1,499/month, Shopify Plus from $2,300/month on a 3-year term, Shopware Evolve from $2,400/month, and B2B Wave at £135/month. Enterprise platforms like OroCommerce start around $22,000/year and scale well beyond that.

### Why won't most B2B platforms publish their pricing?

Many use custom enterprise pricing tied to GMV, order volume or account size. Practically, an unpublished price is rarely a low one, which is useful information when you're deciding where to spend evaluation time.

### What's the best B2B platform for a small distributor?

It depends on whether your sales are catalogue-led or quote-led. For catalogue-led wholesale, BigCommerce B2B and Shopify are the usual shortlist. For quote-led distribution, CPQ-first platforms fit better. For ERP-governed pricing, ERP-led platforms reduce integration work.

### Can I run DTC and wholesale on the same platform?

Yes, and this is a genuine strength of Shopify and BigCommerce — one catalogue, one inventory pool, one order stream. B2B-only platforms generally don't offer this.

### Is OroCommerce or Adobe Commerce worth considering for an SMB?

Both are strong for genuine enterprise complexity, but pricing puts them out of range for most small distributors. Knowing they exist and are not yet your tier saves weeks of evaluation.

### How do I evaluate platforms without wasting months?

Answer four questions first: catalogue-led or quote-led, does your ERP govern pricing, do you sell DTC alongside wholesale, and what's your genuine monthly budget. Those eliminate most of the market before you book a single demo.
