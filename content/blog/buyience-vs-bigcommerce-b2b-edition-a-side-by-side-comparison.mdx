## What BigCommerce B2B Edition actually is

B2B Edition is a layer on top of standard BigCommerce, built partly from two acquisitions: BundleB2B, acquired in April 2022, and B2B Ninja's quoting technology, acquired in February 2022. That origin matters, because it means the B2B capability wasn't retrofitted onto a consumer cart as an afterthought — it was purpose-built B2B software brought in and integrated.

What it includes:

- Company accounts with buyer roles, permissions and self-registration with merchant approval
- Price lists assigning different price sets per company account, manageable via CSV import or API
- Native quoting, including sales-staff-assisted quoting layered on negotiated pricing
- Purchase orders, payment terms and invoices
- A buyer portal for self-service order management, now shipping in 12 languages with a language selector
- A trade professional portal with application and verification, for businesses selling to contractors or architects
- Multi-storefront support, running B2B Edition across several storefronts from one backend
- B2B REST and GraphQL APIs, with quote webhooks covering quote created, updated, status changed and deleted events

That's a serious B2B feature set. Anyone telling you BigCommerce can't do real B2B is working from outdated information. For a broader shortlist of platforms by fit, see our guide to the [best B2B commerce platforms for SMB distributors](/blog/best-b2b-commerce-platforms-for-smb-distributors).

## The cost question that decides most SMB decisions

Here's the thing most comparisons bury: B2B Edition is not available on BigCommerce's standard plans. It's an add-on, available only on the Enterprise tier.

BigCommerce publishes its standard plan pricing — Core at $39/month, Growth at $105, Scale at $399 — but it does not publish Enterprise pricing, and it does not publish B2B Edition pricing. Third-party estimates for Enterprise commonly land in the $1,000–$3,000+/month range, with B2B Edition quoted separately on top, and one TCO analysis estimates $150,000–$400,000 over three years for a mid-market B2B business on BigCommerce with B2B Edition once implementation is included.

Those are third-party figures, not BigCommerce's own, and your actual quote will depend on your revenue and requirements. But the structural point holds regardless of the exact number: you cannot get B2B Edition on an entry plan, and the entry point to the capability is an enterprise sales conversation. See [Buyience pricing](/pricing) for how we approach SMB subscription tiers instead.

There's also an implementation consideration. Custom design work on BigCommerce requires developers familiar with its Stencil framework, which narrows your hiring pool compared with more common front-end stacks.

For a distributor doing £1M–£5M with a handful of reps, this is often the deciding factor — not a feature gap, just a tier mismatch.

## Where the genuine functional difference lies

Setting cost aside, the real distinction between B2B Edition's quoting and what we built isn't whether quoting exists. It's what happens during a negotiation rather than during quote creation.

**Live negotiation versus quote exchange.** B2B Edition supports creating a quote, sending it, and a buyer responding. What it doesn't provide is a shared workspace where both parties work through terms in real time, with versioned revisions and preserved negotiation history. On B2B Edition that back-and-forth typically happens over email alongside the quote. For distributors whose deals involve several rounds on specific line items, that's where version confusion and undocumented concessions originate — the problem a [Digital Sales Room](/digital-sales-room) is built to solve.

**Margin enforcement at the point of concession.** This is the difference that costs real money. When a rep discounts to close, the question is whether the system checks that concession against your actual margin floor *before* the quote is sent, or whether you find out afterwards. Buyience enforces the floor at line level and routes sub-floor pricing for approval. Price lists define what a customer should pay; they don't independently stop a rep going below cost under negotiation pressure.

**Line-level pricing resolution during negotiation.** A multi-line quote where tier, volume break and contract rate resolve differently per line, and a buyer pushes on three lines specifically, is a different problem from applying a company's price list to a cart. See how [customer-specific pricing at scale](/blog/how-b2b-teams-manage-customer-specific-pricing-at-scale) works when those rules have to resolve per line.

These are narrower differences than the gap with a catalogue-first platform — including how we frame [Buyience vs Shopify Plus](/blog/buyience-vs-shopify-plus-for-b2b-which-fits-wholesale-distributors). If your quoting is structured — send a quote, buyer accepts or doesn't — they may not matter to you at all.

## FAQ: BigCommerce B2B Edition vs Buyience

### Does BigCommerce B2B Edition include native quoting?

Yes. B2B Edition includes a native quoting flow, built in part on B2B Ninja quoting technology BigCommerce acquired in February 2022, with sales-staff-assisted quoting layered on negotiated pricing. It is a genuine quoting capability, not a token feature.

### How much does BigCommerce B2B Edition cost?

BigCommerce does not publish a price for B2B Edition or for the Enterprise plan it requires. Third-party estimates commonly place Enterprise in the $1,000–$3,000+/month range with B2B Edition quoted separately on top. Expect a custom quote based on your revenue and requirements.

### Can I get B2B Edition on a standard BigCommerce plan?

No. B2B Edition is only available as an add-on to the Enterprise tier. BigCommerce's published Core, Growth and Scale plans do not include it.

### What's the main functional difference between B2B Edition and Buyience?

Both have native quoting. The differences are live negotiation — B2B Edition has no native shared workspace with versioned revision history — and margin floor enforcement at the point a rep makes a concession, rather than discovering the result afterwards.

### Which is better for a distributor selling both wholesale and direct to consumers?

BigCommerce, clearly. Running B2B and B2C from one catalogue, inventory pool and backend is a structural advantage Buyience doesn't offer.

### Does BigCommerce B2B Edition need specialist developers?

Custom design work on BigCommerce uses its Stencil framework, so front-end customisation requires developers familiar with it. That's worth factoring into implementation cost and hiring if you plan significant customisation.
